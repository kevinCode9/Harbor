# Stage 5 — leftover foundation is not assumed clean

Stage 5 is the backend fix-up. The leftover app under a slice may be rushed, oversized, or have too many writers. Do not treat that base as finished or tidy.

## On every Stage 5 task

1. Work the approved slice only. Smallest correct change.
2. If a leftover foundation file is awkward (huge, limity, leftover mint/cookie paths, competing writers, too many lines for the job), **say so in the reply**: what is weird, how it may force or limit **this slice**, and how it may limit a **later Stage 5/6 goal** (exact-tab, Activity, Send, Presence, Admin, retention). Do not wait for the operator to ask.
3. APPLY is not PASS. A later 5.x slice may still correct the foundation when evidence shows it.

## When leftover cleanup is appropriate

- It **is** the approved slice (stopping GET mint in 5.2 first-entry is the work, not a side tidy).
- It **blocks** the approved slice (the leftover writer would keep winning).
- The operator **explicitly** widens scope after the debt was noted.

## When it is not

- “While we are here” refactors of adjacent files, appearance rewrites, extra helpers, or Domain/ extracts.
- Parked later owners (Activity physical, Send, `enc_key`, clock, placement) pulled into this slice.
- Expanding the current slice to fix noted debt without an explicit operator yes.

Prefer: note it → keep the slice small → own the cleanup in the Stage 5 slice that is meant to fix that part.

## Repair foundations for concrete product reasons

Do not refactor merely because code is old, large or untidy. Do not preserve weak foundations merely because they already exist.

For each proposed foundation change, explain:

- The actual defect, conflicting responsibility or limitation.
- The accepted product requirement it affects.
- Why the change belongs in this checkpoint.
- Why the result will remain useful in the deployed architecture.

If the work belongs elsewhere, name its owning stage/checkpoint and dependency. If it blocks current work, explain the specific blocker and smallest necessary change. Do not silently expand scope.

## Show which files and responsibilities change

When a foundation change is proposed, provide a concise current → proposed ownership map:

- Files/methods being reduced, split, retained or retired.
- Responsibilities removed from each existing file.
- The destination owner and callers that will use it.
- Competing writers or duplicate behavior that will stop.

Do not invent file splits to satisfy this rule. If no split is justified, say so. Moving code into another oversized service or wrapping unchanged competing behavior is not substantive repair.

## Preserve lasting product rules

HTTP and later realtime adapters should invoke shared product rules. Page scripts handle presentation and lifecycle; they must not independently create sessions, grant authority or install competing transport loops.

Do not optimize doomed polling or build elaborate compatibility machinery merely to preserve rushed saved-page internals. Preserve intended appearance and product behavior; replace implementation details when justified.

## Raise supported recommendations and seek useful review

If you identify another foundation, schema, caller or future-compatibility issue, state the evidence, impact, recommendation and owning stage. Distinguish a current blocker from later work.

Ask Astra for an opinion when a meaningful architectural tradeoff needs independent review. Supply the relevant repository facts/excerpts and your recommendation. Do not substitute repeated confirmation requests for engineering judgment, and do not send messages externally without authorization. Prompt format and model routing stay in `chatgpt-pm-model-routing.mdc`.

If repository details are uncertain, identify what must be established. Do not guess. Further inspection must remain within the operator’s authorized scope.

## Distinguish implementation from proof

Separate proposed behavior, inspected findings, automated results and browser evidence. State what the change proves and what remains unverified. File counts, new folders and passing tests alone do not mean the foundation is fixed.

Keep this reporting concise and proportional. It must support progress, not create another full proposal cycle.
